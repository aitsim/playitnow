How to play Neon Onslaught

Move your mouse (or drag your finger on mobile) to steer — your character moves toward the cursor, away from the center of the screen. There's no manual shooting: you automatically fire at the nearest enemy on a timer, so the real skill is positioning and dodging. Defeated enemies drop XP orbs; walk near them to collect. Fill the XP bar to level up and choose one of three random upgrades. The run ends when your HP reaches zero — the goal is simply to survive as long as possible.

Tips & strategies

  • Keep moving, even when it's calm. Standing still makes it easy for the swarm to surround you — drift in slow circles to keep enemies approaching from a manageable angle.
  • Prioritize Fire Rate and Multishot early. More outgoing damage per second clears swarms faster, which matters more early on than raw HP.
  • Save Max HP upgrades for later runs. Once enemies hit harder, a bigger health pool buys you more time to reposition.
  • Don't detour for stray XP orbs. A slightly bigger pickup radius (via the Pickup Radius upgrade) is usually safer than walking toward danger for one orb.
  • Watch for the tougher purple enemies. They're slower but hit harder and take more shots to kill — don't let them corner you against a wall of faster swarmers.

Neon Onslaught — FAQ

How do I attack enemies?

You don't need to — your character automatically fires at the nearest enemy on a timer. Focus on moving and dodging; upgrades like Fire Rate and Multishot make your auto-attacks stronger.

What happens when I level up?

The run pauses and you're offered three random upgrades to choose from — pick one to apply it immediately, then the run resumes.

Is there an end goal?

Not yet — it's an endless survival mode. The difficulty ramps up the longer you last, so the goal is simply to survive as long as possible and reach the highest level you can.

Is Neon Onslaught like Vampire Survivors or other bullet heaven games?

It's built on the same survive-and-upgrade formula popularized by Vampire Survivors and other bullet heaven games, but Neon Onslaught is single-player against AI-controlled enemies, with no online leaderboard yet. If you like wave survival games or auto-battler action in general, the core loop will feel familiar.

Does it work on mobile?

Yes — drag your finger anywhere on the game screen to move, the same as using your mouse on desktop.
